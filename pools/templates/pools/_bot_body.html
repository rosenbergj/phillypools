About our bot

If one of these showed up in your logs, this page is here to tell you who we are, what we fetched and how to make us stop.

Philly Pools (phillypools.app) is a small free site that tracks opening dates, closing dates and hours for Philadelphia's public pools. There are no ads on it and nothing is sold. It's run by one person and a lot of volunteered tips.

Only PhillyPoolsBot is a robot. PhillyPools-Submission is a single fetch of a single URL that somebody pasted into our submit form moments earlier — one page, no link-following. PhillyPools-Admin is a person at a terminal running a one-off job.

What we don't do

  • We don't crawl. The scheduled checks fetch a fixed, hand-picked list of {% if monitored_page_count %}{{ monitored_page_count }} page{{ monitored_page_count|pluralize }}{% else %}pages{% endif %} and follow no links out of them.
  • We read pages; we don't poke at them. No form submissions, no logins, nothing behind authentication, no attempt at anything a browser wouldn't request.
  • We don't republish your page. What reaches the site is a pool's dates and hours, alongside a link back to you as the source.
  • We don't hammer. A failed request is retried at most twice, seconds apart, and then left alone until the next scheduled run.
  • We don't re-download what hasn't changed. Scheduled checks send If-None-Match and If-Modified-Since, so an unchanged page costs you a 304 with an empty body instead of a full render. We ask for the whole page once a day regardless, in case a cached validator has gone stale.

Making us stop

Ask. {{ contact_html|safe }} A person reads it, and we'll skip your site — we have no interest in fetching anything from anyone who'd rather we didn't.

In fairness, you should know that we don't read robots.txt yet. Our scheduled fetches go to a short list of pages chosen by hand rather than anything found by crawling, so there has never been a directive for us to discover — but that's an explanation, not an excuse, and support for it is on the list. Until it lands, a request sent to the link above is what stops us, and it's honored by hand.

Why you're seeing us

Most of our fetches go to Philadelphia Parks & Recreation and to the city's public pool dataset. If we turned up somewhere else, it's almost certainly because you posted something about a specific pool — a hand-lettered sign, an "open late tonight" note — and a visitor sent us the link. That kind of post is often the only place a pool's real hours exist, which is the entire reason this site is here.
